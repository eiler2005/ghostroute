GhostRoute — architecture and recovery

Implemented components and their boundaries. The engineering register owns priorities and evidence completion criteria.

Routing

This is the default split, not every packet-level exception. LAN REDIRECT covers classified managed IPv4 TCP; managed UDP/443 is dropped. anyT projects the selected-client policy into its own process; it does not relay through B or inherit A's selected full-VPS overrides. Its eight issued imports remain unchanged during maintenance. Sensitive applies only to matching requests. Client DIRECT, hidden names and app-owned DNS need separate proof. Native C needs client support; D is a lab. Amn and legacy WireGuard remain disabled with configuration retained. M has an isolated service policy.

Lifecycle ownership

Arrows show lifecycle control, not packet flow. The supervisor invokes anyT's own controller; firewall-start and the minute cron also reconcile it. Healthy missing-firewall repair keeps its processes; changed inputs require validated restart after detection. Polling is not an atomic or zero-downtime guarantee. anyT reuses certificate files maintained by the existing TLS owner. The LAN guard begins after the firewall hook. Console's service key is constrained to fixed reports; the operator key stays on the control machine. Amn requires explicit activation.

Recovery assets

This is the recovery process, not a claim that every external copy has passed it. Include anyT server settings, issued imports, approval manifest, compatible runtime and TLS/hook dependencies. The client-only ZIP cannot restore the server. Compare issued artifacts with the private approval record; do not generate replacement identities. Exact locations, hashes and results remain private. Complete archive reads and rebuilding a spare router are separate evidence levels. Independent password recovery is required for loss of the control machine.
